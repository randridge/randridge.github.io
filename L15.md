Generally speaking, most surveys combine multiple of the features we
have covered:

-   Simple random sampling (without replacement)

-   Stratification

-   Cluster sampling

Many large-scale surveys are where:

1.  PSUs are grouped into strata

2.  PSUs are sampled (often via PPS) independently within each stratum

3.  SSUs are sampled (often via SRS) from within each selected PSU

4.  Tertiary sampling units (TSUs) are sampled from within each selected
    SSU

5.  ...

Technically, you could assemble the various formulae we have used for
each of these different sampling techniques to (hand-)calculate point
estimates and variance estimates for population-level quantities.

(But obviously nobody does this anymore! Thank you, computers.)

**Goal:** Estimate percent of beds using netting (Malaria prevention) in

**Multistage Design with Stratification**

**Stage 1:**

-   Stratify districts by region: Western, Central, Eastern

-   Take a PPS sample of 5 districts within each stratum, with
    population size in each district as the size variable (from national
    census data)

**Stage 2:**

-   Stratify villages by whether or not they had a primary health care
    (PHC) facility

-   Take a PPS sample of 2 villages within each stratum, with population
    size in each village as the size variable (from national census
    data)

**Stage 3:**

-   Select 6 compounds per village via SRS

-   Record the number of beds and how many had nets within each compound

To estimate the total number of beds in rural Gambia that use netting:

**Starting from the last stage and working backwards:**

1.  Record total number of beds with nets for each compound (TSU)

2.  Estimate total number of beds with nets for each village (SSU) by:
    $$(\# \text{ compounds in village}) \times (\text{average number of beds with nets per compound})$$

3.  Estimate the total number of beds with nets by:
    $$\sum_{\text{selected villages in PHC stratum}} \frac{\text{estimated total number of beds for the village}}{\text{probability of selection from PPS}}$$
    Repeat

4.  Estimate the total number of beds with nets in each district (PSU)
    by:
    $$\text{Estimated total in PHC villages} + \text{Estimated total in non-PHC villages}$$

5.  Estimate the total number of beds with nets by:
    $$\sum_{\text{selected districts in Eastern stratum}} \frac{\text{estimated total number of beds for the district}}{\text{probability of selection from PPS}}$$
    Repeat

6.  Estimate overall total by:
    $$\text{Estimated total in Eastern} + \text{Estimated total in Central} + \text{Estimated total in Western}$$

If we can calculate sampling weights then we don't need to go through
the (ugly) process of working backwards through the stages to get to the
ultimate total

Fortunately, the principles of conditional probability allow this to be
doable

Start with the probability of selection: $$\begin{aligned}
P(\text{TSU }k\text{ in SSU }j\text{ in PSU }i\text{ selected}) &= P(\text{PSU }i\text{ selected}) \\
&\quad\times P(\text{SSU }j\text{ selected }|\text{ PSU }i\text{ selected})\\
&\qquad\times P(\text{TSU }k\text{ selected }|\text{ PSU }i\text{ and SSU }j\text{ selected}) \\
\pi_{ijk} &= \pi_{i} \times \pi_{j|i} \times \pi_{k|ij}
\end{aligned}$$

From here obtain the weights:
$$w_{ijk} = \frac{1}{P(\text{TSU }k\text{ in SSU }j\text{ in PSU }i\text{ selected})} = \frac{1}{\pi_{ijk}}$$

These weights (inverse of selection probabilities) are often called the

Often, post-survey adjustments to the weights (e.g., poststratification,
adjustments for nonresponse, etc.) are done to provide the **final
weights** used for analysis

Using you can obtain **point estimates**

As we've seen with all of our designs considered so far, estimates can
be written in terms of in a simple form:
$$\that = \sum_{i \in S} w_i y_i \qquad \qquad \hat{\ybar} = \frac{\that}{\sum_{i \in S} w_i} = \frac{\sum_{i \in S} w_i y_i}{\sum_{i \in S} w_i}$$
Here, $i$ indexes the observation unit (could be PSU, SSU, TSU, etc.)

(This general notation works for any design and avoids complex notation
like $y_{ijk}$)

Note that sampling weights **only need to be calculated for the units
that end up in the sample**

Sampling weights for units **not** in the sample are 0

Note that you can also calculate the for each stage and , because (for a
general case) $$\pi_{ijk} = \pi_{i} \times \pi_{j|i} \times \pi_{k|ij}$$
and thus
$$w_{ijk} = \frac{1}{\pi_{ijk} }= \frac{1}{\pi_{i} \times \pi_{j|i} \times \pi_{k|ij}} = \frac{1}{\pi_{i}} \times \frac{1}{\pi_{j|i}} \times \frac{1}{\pi_{k|ij}} = w_i \times w_{j|i} \times w_{k|ij}$$

For the example, **Young** person in **Region (PSU) 1**:

  ------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PSU 1:                    $\displaystyle w_1 = \frac{1}{\pi_1} = \frac{1}{0.3} = 3.3333$
  Young $j$ within PSU 1:   $\displaystyle w_{j|1}^{Y} = \frac{1}{\pi_{j|1}^{Y}} = \frac{1}{0.0125} = 80$
  Overall weight:           $\displaystyle w_{1j}^{Y}=w_1\times w_{j|1}^{Y} = 3.3333 \times 80 = 266.67$ (same answer)
  ------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose we have a complex design involving:

-   Dividing the population into $H$ : $\red{h}=1,\dots,H$

-   Sampling $n_h$ within stratum $h$: $\blue{i}=1,\dots,n_h$

-   Sampling $m_{hi}$ within PSU $i$ within stratum $h$:
    $\green{j}=1,\dots, m_{hi}$

-   $w_{hij}$ = weight for in in

-   $y_{hij}$ = outcome for in in

Estimated total = sum up the product of the sampling weights $(w_{hij})$
and the outcome $(y_{hij})$ over all the sampled SSUs within each
sampled PSU within each stratum:
$$\that_{HT} = \underbrace{\sum_{h=1}^H}_{\red{strata}} \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{n_h}}_{\blue{PSUs}} \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{m_{hi}}}_{\green{SSUs}} w_{hij} y_{hij}$$
