If $X^2 \sim \chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)}$, then $E[X^2] = (r-1)(c-1)$\
(expected value of a chi-square random variable is equal to its DF)

But, in complex survey designs, $X^2$ from a chi-square test follow a
$\chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)}$ distribution under $H_0$

However, we can multiply the test statistic $X^2$ by factor(s) in order
to make this rescaled test statistics have an approximately $\chi^2$
distribution

Two methods used most often:

-   First Order Correction (also called Rao-Scott first order
    correction)

-   Second Order Correction (also called Rao-Scott second order
    correction)

(not very creatively named)

Option 1: **(Rao-Scott) First Order Correction**

-   Idea: Match the of the test statistic $X^2$ (under the complex
    design) to the of a $\chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)}$ distribution, thus
    "rescaling\" the test statistic

-   Hence the name, **first order** -- matching means\

    -   Calculate $E[X^2]$ = the expected value of $X^2$ for the complex
        design under $H_0$

    -   Calculate first order corrected test statistic as
        $$X^2_F = \frac{(r-1)(c-1) X^2}{E[X^2]}$$

    -   Compare $X^2_F$ to a $\chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)}$ distribution

    -   In practice, works better to compare $X^2_F/(r-1)(c-1)$ to an
        $F$ distribution

Calculation of $E[X^2]$ depends on the cell and marginal probabilities
and the DEFF for estimating each of these probabilities (there's the
survey design part!)

Slight problem: we are only matching the mean of the distribution -- but
p-values often come from the tail of the distribution (at least the
p-values we care about most!)

Variance of $X^2_F$ is actually larger than variance of
$\chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)} \rightarrow$ p-values from $X^2_F$ are slightly
smaller than they should be

Option 2: **(Rao-Scott) Second Order Correction**:

-   Idea: Match the of the test statistic $X^2$ (under the complex
    design) to the of a $\chi^2_{(r-1)(c-1)}$ distribution, thus
    "rescaling\" the test statistic

-   Hence the name, **second order** -- matching means and variances\

    -   Calculate $E[X^2]$ = the expected value of $X^2$ for the complex
        design under $H_0$

    -   Calculate $V[X^2]$ = the variance of $X^2$ under $H_0$

    -   Calculate second order corrected test statistic as
        $$X^2_S = \frac{\nu X^2_F}{(r-1)(c-1)} \quad \text{where } \nu = 2\frac{(E[X^2])^2}{V[X^2]}$$

    -   Compare $X^2_F$ to a $\chi^2_{\nu}$ distribution

    -   In practice, works better to compare $X^2_S/\nu$ to an $F$
        distribution

    -   Similar to the Satterthwaite approximation for ANOVA

Calculation of $V[X^2]$ even more involved than $E[X^2]$, requires
covariance matrix of estimated cell proportions

For many software packages (Stata, R), the second order correction is
the default
