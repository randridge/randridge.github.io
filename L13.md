To estimate the mean of $y$ we\...

-   observe the true $t_i$ (total of $y$ for cluster $i$) in each
    sampled PSU,

-   calculate the average cluster total across the sampled PSUs
    $(\tbar)$,

-   multiply this average by the number of PSUs ($N$) to get a grand
    total $(\thatclus)$

-   and divide by the total number of SSUs $(M_0)$ to get the unbiased
    estimator $(\ybarclus)$

-   OR, divide by the estimated total number of SSUs $(\widehat{M}_0)$
    to get the ratio estimate $(\ybarr)$

In a , **we don't observe the true $\mathbf t_i$** for each sampled PSU

Essentially this is the only change to the estimation process -- add in
a step to and then use them like we used the true totals in one-stage
cluster sampling

In other words, replace $t_i$ with $\that_i$ above (and $\tbar$ with
$\bar{\that}$ )

Of course, variance estimation is much messier...

Start with :

  **Quantity**                          **Estimate**                                                                               **In Words**
  ------------------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PSU Mean (of $y$)                     $\displaystyle \ybar_i= \frac{1}{m_i} \sum_{j\in \S_i} \yij$                               average of $y$ for sampled SSUs in PSU $i$
  PSU Total (of $y$)                    $\displaystyle \that_i = M_i \ybar_i$                                                      average of $y$ for sampled SSUs times the total number of SSUs in PSU $i$
  Variance of $y$ for SSUs within PSU   $\displaystyle s_i^2 = \frac{1}{m_i-1} \sum_{j \in \S_i} \left(\yij -  \ybar_i\right)^2$   variance of $y$ for sampled SSUs in PSU $i$

These look messy -- but are just the applied !

Note that the total, $\that_i$, has a "hat\" on it -- it is an
**estimate**, just like the mean and variance, since we didn't observe
all the SSUs (different from 1-stage sampling)

If we have taken an SRS within PSU, then (by properties of SRS):

-   Estimated PSU mean is unbiased: $E[\ybar_i] = \ybar_{Ui}$

-   Estimated PSU total is unbiased: $E[\that_i] = t_i$

-   Estimated variance of $y$ within PSU is unbiased: $E[s_i^2] = S_i^2$

Then combine to get :

  **Quantity**              **Estimate**                                                                                  **In Words**
  ------------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------
  Mean PSU Total (of $y$)   $\displaystyle \bar{\that} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i \in \S} \that_i$                             average of estimated PSU totals from sampled PSUs
  Overall Total (of $y$)    $\displaystyle \thatclus = N\bar{\that}$                                                      estimated average PSU total times the number of PSUs (in pop.)
  Overall Mean (of $y$)     $\displaystyle \ybarclus = \frac{\thatclus}{M_0}$ (unbiased estimator)                        estimated total divided by **known** total number of SSUs
  2-3                       $\displaystyle \ybarr = \frac{\thatclus}{\widehat{M}_0}$ (ratio estimator)                    estimated total divided by **estimated** total number of SSUs
  Variance of PSU Totals    $\displaystyle s^2_t = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{i \in \S} \left(\that_i - \bar{\that} \right)^2$   variance of $\that_i$ for sampled PSUs

Note that these steps look just like the steps for one-stage cluster
samples -- but with (additional) "hats\" on the pieces involving $t$
(e.g., $\that_i, \bar{\that}$ )

So to estimate the mean of $y$ we\...

-   calculate the mean of $y$ for each sampled PSU $(\ybar_i)$,

-   multiply this average by the number of SSUs per PSU ($M_i$) to get
    the estimated totals in each PSU $(\that_i)$

-   calculate the average of the estimated cluster totals $\that_i$
    across the sampled PSUs $(\bar{\that})$,

-   multiply this average by the number of PSUs ($N$) to get a grand
    total $(\thatclus)$

-   and divide by the total number of SSUs $(M_0)$ to get the unbiased
    estimate $(\ybarclus)$

-   OR, divide by the estimated total number of SSUs $(\widehat{M}_0)$
    to get the ratio estimate $(\ybarr)$

As we shall show, $\thatclus$ is an of the true population total $t_U$

And thus, as its name suggests, the unbiased estimator of the mean
$(\ybarclus = \thatclus/M_0)$ is an of the true population mean,
$\ybar_U$

-   $E[\thatclus] = t_U$

-   $E[\ybarclus] = \ybar_U$

But the ratio estimate of the mean $(\ybarr = \thatclus/\widehat{M}_0)$
is , just like with 1-stage cluster sampling

-   $E[\ybarr] \ne \ybar_U$

However, as in that scenario, the drastic decrease in variance for
$\ybarr$ outweighs its small bias, making it the preferred estimator

For multi-stage sampling we prove theoretical results by **conditioning
on a realized sample** and then taking the expectation -- using the
property of successive conditioning: $E(A) = E[E(A|B)]$

Inclusion indicator $Z_i$ is a random variable ($Z_i = 1$ if PSU $i$ is
in the sample, 0 otherwise):

For equal probability sampling of PSUs,\
$P(\text{PSU $i$ included in sample}) = P(Z_i=1) = \pi_i = n/N = E(Z_i) = E(Z_i^2)$

Thus we have: $$\begin{aligned}
    E(\thatclus) = E(N\bar{\that}~) = E \left(N \sum_{i \in \S} \frac{1}{n} \that_i \right) &= E \left(\sum_{i = 1}^N Z_i \frac{N}{n} \that_i \right) = E \left[ E \left(\sum_{i = 1}^N Z_i \frac{N}{n}\that_i  \bigg| Z_1, \dots, Z_N \right) \right] \\
    &= E \left[ \sum_{i = 1}^N Z_i \frac{N}{n} E\left(\that_i  \bigg| Z_1, \dots, Z_N \right) \right] \\
    &= E \left[ \sum_{i = 1}^N Z_i \frac{N}{n} E\left(\that_i \right) \right] \quad \text{because } \that_i \perp (Z_1,\dots,Z_N)^*\\
    &= E \left( \sum_{i = 1}^N Z_i \frac{N}{n} t_i \right) \quad \text{because } E(\that_i) = t_i \\
    &= \sum_{i = 1}^N E(Z_i) \frac{N}{n} t_i \\
    &= \sum_{i = 1}^N \frac{n}{N} \frac{N}{n} t_i = \sum_{i = 1}^N t_i = t_U \quad \text{unbiased!}
\end{aligned}$$

Thus,
$E(\ybarclus)  = E ( \thatclus / M_0) = E ( \thatclus)/M_0 = t_U/M_0 = \ybar_U$
Unbiased!

Before we dive into the variance estimation for two-stage cluster
sampling, a note:

We will spend a bit of time calculating the **variance of the estimated
total**\
(and how to estimate it)

But -- our main interest is often **not** in the totals!

(Possible exception: estimating the number of people with a condition)

So why do we focus on the total?

Nearly every quantity we want to estimate can be written as a function
of **totals**

If we can write the quantity in terms of totals, we can use
**linearization** to estimate its variance -- like we did with the ratio
estimate of the mean in 1-stage cluster sampling

Linearization can be used for a wide range of estimates:

-   Means (as ratios)

-   Domain (subgroup) estimates

-   Linear regression coefficients

-   Logistic regression coefficients

-   etc.

As it turns out, $V(\thatclus)$ under two-stage cluster sampling is
equal to:

-   the variance under one-stage cluster sampling, **plus**

-   an extra term to account for the fact that we are the $\that_i$\
    (as opposed to directly observing the true $t_i$ in one-stage
    cluster sampling)

True (theoretical) variance of the estimated total[^2]:
$$V(\thatclus) = \underbrace{N^2 \fpcpar \frac{S^2_t}{n}}_{V_{stage1}} + \underbrace{\frac{N}{n} \sum_{i=1}^N M_i^2 \left(1-\frac{m_i}{M_i}\right) \frac{S_i^2}{m_i}}_{V_{stage2}}$$
where:\
$S^2_t$ = true (population) variance of the PSU totals, $t_i$\
$S_i^2$ = true variance of the $\yij$ within PSU $i$

Note that both these pieces look like SRS formulae -- we take an SRS at
each stage.

That is the **theoretical** variance; we need an estimator

Replacing the unknown variances $S_t^2$ and $S_i^2$ with their sample
estimates gives us:

$$\widehat{V}(\thatclus) = \underbrace{N^2 \fpcpar \frac{s^2_t}{n}}_{\widehat{V}_{stage1}} + \underbrace{\frac{N}{n} \sum_{i \in \S} \left(1-\frac{m_i}{M_i}\right)M_i^2 \frac{s_i^2}{m_i}}_{\widehat{V}_{stage2}}$$

-   Same as the variance formula for one-stage cluster sampling!

<!-- -->

-   Note that if all SSUs are selected (as in one-stage cluster
    sampling), $m_i=M_i$ and this second piece is 0

In many (most) situations, when $N$ is large (and FPC small),
$\widehat{V}_{stage1} >> \widehat{V}_{stage2}$

-   The variance of the $\that_i$ can be very large since it is affected
    by differences in cluster sizes $(M_i)$ and by differences in
    cluster means $(\ybar_i)$

-   Even if clusters have similar means, different sizes will lead to
    the stage one component of variance being large (and dominating the
    second stage piece)
